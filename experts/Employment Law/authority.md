## Wages, final pay, and retaliation

- **Verified source reading, 2026-08-23.** C.R.S. § 8-4-101(5) defines an employee as a person providing labor or services for an employer's benefit. Control and whether the work is the employer's primary work are relevant. A person primarily free of control and customarily engaged in an independent trade related to the service falls outside the definition. Section 8-4-101(6) uses the FLSA § 203(d) employer definition with listed additions. [Colorado Public Law, citing the official 2024 Title 8 PDF](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_8-4-101).
- **Unresolved owner liability.** HB 25-1001, effective 2025-08-06, was reported to add individuals who own or control at least 25% of the ownership interests, with a delegation exception. CDLE INFO #11A, 2025-08-07, describes owners of at least 50% as employers and those with 25% to 49.9% as employers unless they fully delegated day-to-day control. Official 2025 text and its application before naming an owner personally `[verify]`.
- **Verified source reading, 2026-08-23.** C.R.S. § 8-4-101(14)(a)(I) covers labor or service amounts once earned, vested, and determinable. Subsection (II) includes bonuses and commissions earned under an agreement. Subsection (III) requires an employer that offers paid vacation to pay earned and determinable vacation on separation under the agreement. Subsection (14)(b) excludes severance pay from wages or compensation. [Colorado Public Law, citing the official 2024 Title 8 PDF](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_8-4-101).
- **Verified source reading, 2026-08-23.** C.R.S. § 8-4-109(1)(a) makes earned unpaid wages due immediately on discharge, subject to short payroll-unit exceptions. Subsection (1)(b) uses the next regular payday when a worker quits. Subsection (2) excludes pay not yet fully earned. Under subsection (3)(b), failure to pay all earned, vested, and determinable wages in the 14 days after written demand, civil action, or a CDLE claim triggers an automatic penalty of twice the unpaid wages or $1,000, or three times the unpaid wages or $3,000 for a willful violation. [Colorado Public Law, citing the official 2024 Title 8 PDF](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_8-4-109).
- **Verified source reading, 2026-08-23.** Under C.R.S. § 8-4-110, the employee may recover fees if the recovery beats the employer's tender. The employer may recover fees if a timely tender equals or exceeds the recovery. Subsection (2) permits suit in a competent court without exhausting CDLE. CDLE is an optional parallel route for the wage component. The reported CDLE administrative cap was expected to rise toward $13,000 on 2026-07-01 under HB 25-1001. Current cap `[verify]`. A large dispute over contractual severance calls for a separate forum and contract assessment. [Colorado Public Law, citing the official 2024 Title 8 PDF](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_8-4-110).
- **Verified source reading of 2024 text, 2026-08-23.** C.R.S. § 8-4-120 prohibits intimidation, threats, restraint, coercion, blacklisting, discharge, discrimination and retaliation against workers for complaints, proceedings or actual or potential testimony under wage or hours laws. The remedies include back pay, reinstatement or front pay, unpaid wages, 12% interest, a $50 daily penalty, liquidated damages of twice unpaid wages or $2,000, injunctive relief, and mandatory fees for a prevailing employee. The 2024 text also identifies a class 2 misdemeanor. Justia's 2025 compilation listed compensatory damages and a 90-day temporal inference with HB 25-1001 as a possible basis. The official 2025 text is `[verify]` before either addition is pleaded. [Colorado Public Law, 2024 text](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_8-4-120).
- **Verified source reading, 2026-08-23.** Waiver or modification of wage rights in violation of Article 4 is void under C.R.S. § 8-4-121. The limitations periods recorded under § 8-4-122 are two years and three years for willful violations. [Section 8-4-121](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_8-4-121), [Section 8-4-122](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_8-4-122).
- **Verified source reading, holding quoted 2026-08-23.** The wage statute creates no entitlement to vacation. When an employer offers vacation, earned and determinable vacation cannot be forfeited. *Nieto v. Clark's Market, Inc.*, 2021 CO 48, ¶ 3, 488 P.3d 1140 (Colo. 2021). [Colorado Supreme Court opinion text on Justia](https://law.justia.com/cases/colorado/supreme-court/2021/2021-co-48.html). A nominally unlimited or no-accrual PTO plan raises a fact question about whether vacation was earned. Read the full CourtListener opinion before relying on it in a filing.
- **Claim lens.** The wage record needs the worker and employer, earned, vested, determinable unpaid wages apart from severance, how separation occurred and the corresponding due date, nonpayment, and the demand, 14-day period, penalty, and fee issues. Bonus entitlement turns on earning under the agreement. C.R.S. §§ 8-4-101(14), 8-4-109, 8-4-110. [Official Colorado General Assembly 2024 Title 8 PDF cited by the statute pages](https://leg.colorado.gov/sites/default/files/images/olls/crs2024-title-08.pdf).

## Discharge, Cause, and contract remedies

- **Verified.** Breach of an employment contract, including a contractual severance promise, uses the same four contract elements as other Colorado contracts. The claimant needs a contract, performance or justification for nonperformance, nonperformance by the other party, and damages. *Western Distributing Co. v. Diodosio*, 841 P.2d 1053, 1058 (Colo. 1992). [Contract opinion verification and limitations authorities](../Contract%20Disputes/authority.md). The same reference records three years for general contract actions under C.R.S. § 13-80-101(1)(a), and six years under § 13-80-103.5(1)(a) for covered liquidated or determinable money claims, subject to its exceptions and accrual check.
- **At-will and express pay.** The 2026-08-23 reading used Colorado Civil Jury Instruction 31:5 notes. The described at-will default is indefinite employment, terminable by either side without notice or cause when no agreement limits the right to discharge. The notes generally recognize no liability absent special consideration or an agreement to the contrary. A written payment promise for termination without Cause may govern the financial consequences of discharge while leaving the relationship at will. Current CJI text and packet `[verify]`. [Colorado civil jury instructions packet](https://www.coloradojudicial.gov/media/8653).
- **Statutory classification of severance.** C.R.S. § 8-4-101(14)(b) excludes severance pay from wages. Summaries report that *Sommers v. MarketPlace Realty, LLC*, 2025 COA 97 (Colo. App. 2025-12-24), applied this exclusion to severance compensation under an employment agreement payable on termination. Justia and Colorado Lawyer summaries read 2026-08-23. Opinion `[verify]`. This classification leaves the express-contract issue for separate analysis.
- **Notice and cure.** When the actual contract defines a performance-based Cause prong to require written notice and a reasonable opportunity to cure a curable failure, read the proviso as part of that prong and test the whole agreement. A Colorado employment opinion applying that rule and giving effect to the cure clause `[verify]`. Check whether there was written notice, a material duty and specific failure the worker could identify and fix, a reasonable period for an effective cure, and continued failure after the period as the stated ground. A completed crime or fraud is an example of potentially incurable conduct to assess under a distinct available prong and its proof. A coaching message, HR concerns note, or memorializing email that supplies no deadline, Cause ground, or specific cure is weak evidence of notice under the checklist. The checklist is contract analysis rather than an opinion holding.
- **Public-policy wrongful discharge.** For the at-will exception in *Martin Marietta Corp. v. Lorenz*, 823 P.2d 100 (Colo. 1992), screen for an employer directing an illegal act at work or prohibiting a public duty or important job-related right, a specific public health, safety, or welfare statute or clear public policy, discharge because of refusal, and employer knowledge, actual or constructive, of the worker's reasonable belief that the act was unlawful. Opinion `[verify]`. A general claim of unfairness does not supply those facts. Check the reported self-defense extension in *Moreno v. Circle K Stores, Inc.* (Colo. 2026-06-15) `[verify]`. Reliance on that extension for an unrelated workplace dispute requires separate authority.
- **Handbook and implied contract.** Check the requirement of employer intent to be bound by handbook procedures in *Continental Air Lines, Inc. v. Keenan*, 731 P.2d 708 (Colo. 1987) `[verify]`, and consideration of the entire policy including disclaimers in *Collins v. Colorado Mountain College*, 56 P.3d 1132 (Colo. App. 2002) `[verify]`. Check the defense based on after-acquired application fraud in *Crawford Rehabilitation Services, Inc. v. Weissman*, 938 P.2d 540 (Colo. 1997) `[verify]`. A disclaimer and clause reserving changes to an executive's written agreement point toward checking that writing, including a signed offer and performance under it, before treating a handbook as a for-Cause contract.
- **Verified. Promissory estoppel.** A disputed writing or signature can make the reliance theory relevant. Check for a promise, action or forbearance of a definite and substantial character, and injustice avoidable only by enforcement. *Kiely v. St. Germain*, 670 P.2d 764 (Colo. 1983). [Contract opinion verification and scope](../Contract%20Disputes/authority.md).
- **Constructive discharge.** Check for deliberate action making or allowing conditions to become so intolerable that a reasonable person in the employee's position would have no choice but to resign. *Montemayor v. Jacor Communications, Inc.*, 64 P.3d 916, 922 (Colo. App. 2002), holding and pinpoint `[verify]`. The standard concerns a reasonable employee's position. *Christie v. San Miguel County School District*, 759 P.2d 779, 783 (Colo. App. 1998) `[verify]`. Related authority *Wilson v. Board of County Commissioners*, 703 P.2d 1257 (Colo. 1985) `[verify]`. Hostility, second-guessing and stress alone do not establish intolerable conditions. Before treating resignation as a route to a claim, check whether the severance condition requires employer termination without Cause.
- **Good faith.** Check the proposition that an implied covenant does not make an at-will termination a for-Cause decision against *Decker v. Browning-Ferris Industries of Colorado, Inc.*, 931 P.2d 436 (Colo. 1997) `[verify]`. Express Cause, severance, and good-faith bonus determination terms raise issues under the writing itself.
- **Prevention of a condition.** Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 245 states the principle that a party cannot prevent the other party from fulfilling a condition and then rely on its failure. Find a Colorado employment opinion applying § 245 where dismissal before a measurement date prevents an executive bonus determination `[verify]`. Evidence includes who controlled the measurement, a required joint confirmation, and any express good-faith limit on a final and binding determination. The verified *Taylor* expectation measure and *Kiely* rule against misuse of the statute of frauds address distinct propositions. [Contract opinion verification and scope](../Contract%20Disputes/authority.md).

## Restrictions, forum, and arbitration

- **Verified source reading, 2026-08-23.** C.R.S. § 8-2-113(2)(a) generally voids noncompetes restricting receipt of compensation for labor, with exceptions. Subsection (2)(b) identifies the highly compensated worker exception to protect trade secrets where it goes no further than reasonably necessary. Subsection (2)(d) identifies the customer nonsolicit exception at 60% of the highly compensated threshold, still tailored to trade secrets. Subsection (3)(b) permits reasonable confidentiality that leaves general skills, public information, and legally protected disclosures accessible. Subsection (4) calls for separate signed notice before hire or 14 days before a mid-employment covenant. Subsection (6) bars out-of-state adjudication for a worker primarily residing or working in Colorado at termination and applies Colorado enforceability law despite contrary terms. Subsection (8) identifies actual damages, $5,000 per worker, and fees for presenting or enforcing a void covenant. Check whether presenting a void nonsolicit as an employment condition triggers subsection (8). [Colorado Public Law, C.R.S. § 8-2-113](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_8-2-113).
- **Verified source reading, 2026-08-23.** Transaction of business in Colorado can establish jurisdiction for a cause arising from that transaction under C.R.S. § 13-1-124(1)(a). Before applying the statute to hiring a Colorado-based remote worker, check the offer's place-of-work term and paid work there, and find a case applying the statute to remote employment `[verify]`. [Colorado Public Law, C.R.S. § 13-1-124](https://colorado.public.law/statutes/crs_13-1-124).
- **Venue and filing.** Check where the claim arose under C.R.C.P. 98 using the work location and actual county. Confirm the home-office address’s county when a town straddles county lines, as Windsor does across Weld and Larimer counties. The corporate and claim-arose subsections are `[verify]`. Declaratory relief leads are C.R.C.P. 57 and C.R.S. § 13-51-101 et seq. `[verify form]`. Confirm the JDF 601 cover sheet, JDF 600 summons and current local district filing fee `[verify forms and fee]`. Screen district court for claims over $25,000 and possible removal to the federal District of Colorado given diversity and more than $75,000. Use those figures as working thresholds and confirm against the current rule before filing. Check the case-specific removal requirements.
- **Governing law.** A choice-of-law clause with a home-state exception needs its own reading for the law applicable to the contract claim. Forum selection is a separate clause question. The wage nonwaiver rule in § 8-4-121 and the Colorado enforceability and adjudication terms in § 8-2-113(6) are distinct from the law applicable to Cause and severance. Check Colorado public policy and the contract’s home-state exception as potential grounds. Compare Colorado and Florida at-will rules and check Florida Cause and severance law if Florida law applies `[verify]`.
- **Arbitration.** Use the FAA and general enforceability of signed agreements covering employment claims as working checks. Verify *Epic Systems* for class waivers `[verify]`. For an unsigned side agreement referenced in an offer or entire-agreement clause, test contract formation, whether the document was available, the parties' assent to incorporation and terms, and forum assent. Colorado employment authority on unsigned arbitration and incorporation by reference `[verify]`. A new arbitration or supersession form presented under pressure fits the counsel stop.

## Common defenses and open briefing

Assess the at-will default against written post-termination pay, the employer's facts against each numbered Cause prong and any contractual cure requirement, and after-acquired evidence against *Crawford* `[verify]`. For arbitration, forum and choice of law, check signatures, wage nonwaiver and § 8-2-113(6). For a release at age 40 or older, check the OWBPA `[verify]` and same-day signing as a counsel issue. A release is considered in a voluntary settlement and is separate from proof of Cause. Failure to mitigate may be raised against ongoing pay. Check whether a fixed lump-sum severance is a weaker mitigation target `[verify]`. The other defenses to test are the severance classification under *Sommers* `[verify]`, the handbook disclaimer, any express good-faith limit on final bonus determination, and whether an asserted confidentiality duty or material breach has support in a signed agreement or trade-secret evidence.

# Colorado contract authority

Colorado is the anchor jurisdiction. Coverage was substantial as of 2026-05-23. **Verified** refers to the historical source check listed under Provenance. It carries the source and date, and leaves current applicability and filing verification to the expert's gate. Items marked `[verify]` are open.

## Limitations

- **Verified.** C.R.S. § 13-80-101(1)(a) sets a three-year period from accrual for contract actions, including personal contracts and UCC actions, except as provided in § 13-80-103.5. Official Colorado General Assembly 2024 Title 13 PDF, page 609.
- **Verified.** C.R.S. § 13-80-103.5(1)(a) sets six years from accrual for recovery of liquidated debt or an unliquidated but determinable amount, enforcement of rights in an instrument securing payment or evidencing debt, and replevin of personal property encumbered under a debt security instrument. It specifies one year for actions under § 38-35-124.5(3). Title 13 PDF, page 612.
- Accrual on a particular claim depends on its facts. The governing accrual analysis for that claim remains `[verify]`.

## Breach and formation

- **Verified.** A contract claim requires an existing contract, plaintiff performance or justification for nonperformance, defendant nonperformance, and resulting plaintiff damages. Performance means substantial performance. Minor deviations can remain where the defendant received substantially what the parties contracted for. *Western Distributing Co. v. Diodosio*, 841 P.2d 1053, 1058 (Colo. 1992). Verification used CourtListener cluster and opinion 1236402 and the opinion HTML with citations.
- **Verified.** For goods, an offer ordinarily invites acceptance by any manner and medium reasonable in the circumstances unless the language or circumstances unambiguously indicate otherwise. When beginning performance is a reasonable acceptance mode, an offeror who receives no notice within a reasonable time may treat the offer as having lapsed before acceptance. C.R.S. § 4-2-206, including subsection (1), Title 4 PDF, page 18.
- **Verified.** For non-goods breach, the claimant must show an existing agreement and the other side's failure to perform a term. A replacement insurance policy face sheet sent without intent to create a new contract did not create a new twenty-day review agreement. *Coors v. Security Life of Denver Ins. Co.*, 91 P.3d 393, 402 (Colo. App. 2003).
- **Verified.** A purchase-and-sale contract forms when both parties agree on the terms and undertake obligations under them. *Clark v. Scena*, 83 P.3d 1191, 1194 (Colo. App. 2003).
- **Verified.** The essentials of a non-goods contract are competent parties, subject matter, legal consideration, mutual agreement, and mutual obligation. A contract creates an obligation. *Denver Truck Exch. & State Compensation Ins. Fund v. Perryman*, 134 Colo. 586, 592, 307 P.2d 805, 810 (Colo. 1957).
- **Verified.** Every UCC contract or duty carries good faith in performance and enforcement. The duty concerns performance and enforcement. C.R.S. § 4-1-304, Title 4 PDF, page 11.

## Writing and interpretation

- **Verified.** C.R.S. § 38-10-112(1) requires the agreement, note, or memorandum to be in writing and subscribed by the party charged for covered non-goods agreements, on pain of voidness. Covered terms include agreements that cannot be performed within one year of making, special promises to answer for another's debt, default, or miscarriage, and promises made in consideration of marriage except mutual promises to marry. Goods sales and leases are excepted to the extent governed by §§ 4-2-201 and 4-2.5-201. Official Colorado General Assembly 2024 Title 38 PDF, page 76.
- **Verified.** A goods sale at $500 or more generally requires a signed record sufficient to indicate a contract for sale. The section includes merchant-confirmation and part-performance exceptions. C.R.S. § 4-2-201(1) to (3), Title 4 PDF, page 17. Lack of the qualifying writing can be asserted as a defense under the applicable statute.
- **Verified.** For goods, a prior or contemporaneous oral agreement cannot contradict final-record terms. Course of dealing, usage of trade, course of performance, and consistent additional terms can explain or supplement the terms, subject to the complete-and-exclusive statement exception for additional terms. C.R.S. § 4-2-202, Title 4 PDF, pages 17 to 18.
- **Verified.** Parol evidence may clarify an ambiguous instrument about the personal liability of a purported agent, with the whole agreement considered alongside the signature. *Fink v. Montgomery Elevator Co. of Colo.*, 161 Colo. 342, 347, 421 P.2d 735, 737 (Colo. 1966). The trial court may admit parol evidence clarifying the parties' intention where written authorization uses ambiguous wording such as "supporting papers." *Taylor v. Colorado State Bank of Denver*, 165 Colo. 576, 578, 440 P.2d 772, 773 (Colo. 1968).

## Impossibility, waiver, and estoppel

- **Verified.** Impossibility is a defense. Colorado adopted Restatement § 454's practical meaning, which includes impracticability caused by extreme and unreasonable difficulty, expense, injury, or loss. Absolute impossibility is unnecessary. The defense is unavailable where the contract manifests a contrary intention. *City of Littleton v. Employers Fire Ins. Co.*, 169 Colo. 104, 107, 111, 453 P.2d 810, 813, 818 (Colo. 1969), treating Restatement §§ 454, 456 and 457.
- **Verified.** After an alleged UCC breach, the aggrieved party may agree in a signed record to discharge a resulting claim or right in whole or part without consideration. C.R.S. § 4-1-306, Title 4 PDF, page 11.
- **Verified.** Colorado recognizes promissory estoppel under Restatement (Second) of Contracts § 90(1). It can support enforcement of an oral promise despite the statute of frauds, subject to § 139 balancing. Equitable estoppel, part performance, and promissory estoppel can prevent abuse of the statute of frauds. *Kiely v. St. Germain*, 670 P.2d 764, 767 to 770 (Colo. 1983).

## Damages and restitution

- **Verified. Expectation.** The general contract measure compensates the injured party to put them in the position performance would have produced. *Taylor*, 165 Colo. at 581, 440 P.2d at 774. The UCC directs liberal administration of remedies toward the position the aggrieved party would occupy after full performance. C.R.S. § 4-1-305(a), Title 4 PDF, page 11.
- **Reliance.** A Colorado opinion supporting general reliance or out-of-pocket damages as an alternative contract measure outside UCC Article 2 remains `[verify]`. The UCC sections read do not use that label.
- **Verified. Restitution.** When a goods seller justifiably withholds delivery because the buyer breached, the buyer may recover payments subject to the subsection's limits, including liquidated amounts and incidental damages. C.R.S. § 4-2-718(2), Title 4 PDF, page 49. General unjust enrichment requires that the defendant received a benefit at the plaintiff's expense under circumstances making retention without payment unjust. It is quasi-contract, also called contract implied in law. *Salzman v. Bachrach*, 996 P.2d 1263, 1266 (Colo. 2000).
- **Verified. Consequential damages for goods.** The measure includes losses from general or particular requirements and needs the seller had reason to know at contracting and which cover or other measures could not reasonably prevent. It also includes injury to persons or property proximately caused by breach of warranty. C.R.S. § 4-2-715(2), Title 4 PDF, page 49. Consequential damages are available only as specifically provided in the UCC or other law. C.R.S. § 4-1-305(a), Title 4 PDF, page 11.
- **Verified. General and special damages for other contracts.** General damages flow naturally from breach. Special or consequential damages are other foreseeable losses within the parties' reasonable contemplation when they contracted. *Giampapa v. American Family Mut. Ins. Co.*, 64 P.3d 230, 241 (Colo. 2003). The opinion adds requirements for noneconomic damages in the willful-and-wanton insurer-breach setting. Applicability beyond the general or special damages distinction requires a scope check. Adoption of *Hadley v. Baxendale* by name remains `[verify]`. The *Giampapa* passage read uses reasonable-contemplation language and does not cite *Hadley* there.
- **Verified. Liquidated damages for goods.** Reasonableness depends on anticipated or actual harm, proof difficulties, and the inconvenience of securing an adequate remedy. An unreasonably large amount is void as a penalty. C.R.S. § 4-2-718(1), Title 4 PDF, page 49.
- **Verified. Liquidated damages for other contracts.** Parties must intend to liquidate damages, and at contracting the amount must be a reasonable estimate of presumed actual damages and actual damages must be hard to ascertain. An unmet element makes the provision an invalid penalty. A permissible election between actual and liquidated damages must be exclusive. *Ravenstar, LLC v. One Ski Hill Place, LLC*, 2017 CO 83, ¶ 10, ¶¶ 15 to 16, 401 P.3d 552, 555 (Colo. 2017), citing *Klinger v. Adams Cty. Sch. Dist. No. 50*, 130 P.3d 1027, 1034 (Colo. 2006) on the elements.

## Specific performance and fees

- **Verified.** Specific performance is available for unique goods or in other proper circumstances. The decree may set terms for payment, damages, or other relief. C.R.S. § 4-2-716(1) to (2), Title 4 PDF, page 49.
- **Verified.** For non-goods contracts, equitable specific performance requires the claimant to do equity, have clean hands, and be free of the first substantial breach. *Clark*, 83 P.3d at 1193, citing *Golden Press, Inc. v. Rylands*, 124 Colo. 122, 126, 235 P.2d 592, 595 (1951), and *Rolfes v. O'Connor*, 844 P.2d 1330, 1333 (Colo. App. 1992). A real-estate buyer must show willingness and ability to perform. Strict closing tender is a factor that need not control. *Clark*, 83 P.3d at 1194 to 1195.
- **Verified.** Under the American rule, parties ordinarily bear their own fees in contract and tort absent an express statute, rule, or contract provision. Parties may contract otherwise expressly. *Allstate Ins. Co. v. Huizar*, 52 P.3d 816, 818 to 819 (Colo. 2002). C.R.S. § 13-17-102(1) supplies a separate statutory framework for defined circumstances. Title 13 PDF, page 236.
- **Verified.** An example of a trial court awarding fees under an express agreement is the $102,267.75 judgment reported in *McDonald v. Zions First Nat'l Bank, N.A.*, 2015 COA 29, ¶ 7, 348 P.3d 957 (Colo. App. 2015).

Nothing in the record supports the trial court's finding that a valid agreement created a new twenty-day right to review the existing 1994 policy. If Security Life sent the new face sheet as an attempt to satisfy a regulatory inquiry, it did not constitute a valid offer because there was no intent to enter a contract. In contrast, if Security Life sent the face page as an offer to "unilaterally change the contract," Coors rejected that offer when he tendered the policy for a refund. Thus, there is no evidence that Security Life offered, and Coors accepted, an opportunity essentially to rescind the policy and be repaid the premiums.

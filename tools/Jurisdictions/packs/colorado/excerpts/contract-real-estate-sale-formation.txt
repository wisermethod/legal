However, in purchase and sale agreements, unlike in option contracts, a valid contract to buy is formed when both parties have agreed to the terms and have undertaken obligations pursuant to these terms. See Stelson v. Haigler, 63 Colo. 200, 208, 165 P. 265, 268 (1917)("A contract of sale creates mutual obligations on the part of the seller to sell, and on the part of the purchaser to buy, while an option gives the right to purchase, within a limited time, without imposing any obligations to purchase."). See generally Denver Truck Exch. v. Perryman, 134 Colo. 586, 592, 307 P.2d 805, 810 (1957) (essential elements of a contract are "competent parties, subject matter, a legal consideration, mutuality of agreement, and mutuality of obligations"). As a result, a buyer's actions under the terms of the contract must be viewed in light of the seller's conduct. Whether the purchase price was tendered or whether the buyer notified the seller of its intention to be bound, therefore, is not determinative of the buyer's right to specific performance. See Coppom v. Humphreys, supra; Poznik v. Urton & Co., supra.
